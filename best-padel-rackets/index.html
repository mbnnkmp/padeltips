How to choose a padel racket

Four things decide how a racket plays: its shape, its weight and balance, its core and its face. Get the shape right first. Everything else is fine-tuning.

Weight and balance

Most adult rackets weigh roughly 350 to 375 grams. A lighter racket is quicker at the net and easier on the arm. A heavier one feels more stable and hits harder, but tires your forearm faster. A head-heavy balance adds power and adds strain too.

Core: soft or hard

The core is the foam inside the racket. Soft cores (often labelled soft EVA or foam) give you more help on slow balls and absorb more vibration. Hard cores reward a fast swing with more power and precision, but punish mistimed shots and feel harsher on the elbow.

Face: fibreglass or carbon

Fibreglass faces are flexible and forgiving. Carbon faces are stiffer and more precise. You'll see labels like 3K, 12K and 18K, which describe the carbon weave. A higher number usually means a stiffer face. Beginners rarely need carbon.

Mind your elbow. Softer cores, fibreglass faces and a relaxed grip are all kinder to your arm than a stiff carbon racket. If you already have elbow pain, read padel elbow: causes and the rackets that help.

Common racket-buying mistakes

  • Buying the pros' racket too early. Pro rackets are usually stiff, head-heavy diamonds. They're hard to control at club level.
  • Going too heavy. A heavy racket feels solid in the shop and exhausting by the third set.
  • Ignoring comfort. Elbow pain is one of the most common padel injuries. A softer racket is cheap insurance.
  • Not trying first. Many clubs have demo rackets. A few games tell you more than any spec sheet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best padel racket for a beginner?

A round racket with a soft core and a light, low balance. It gives you the biggest sweet spot and the most control. Good examples are the Nox X-Hero 2026, Bullpadel Indiga Control 2026 and Head Evo Extreme.

How much should I spend on my first padel racket?

You don't need a flagship racket to start. A mid-priced beginner racket is lighter, softer and more forgiving than most expensive pro models, which are built for advanced players.

Is a heavier padel racket better?

Not usually. A heavier racket adds stability and power, but it's slower at the net and puts more strain on your arm. Most club players are better off with a lighter racket.

When should I upgrade my padel racket?

Upgrade when your racket is holding you back: you're consistently looking for more power on smashes, or you've outgrown its control. Many players know within their first year.

Round, teardrop or diamond: which is best?

Round for control and beginners, teardrop for all-round play, and diamond for power. See our guide to padel racket shapes.
