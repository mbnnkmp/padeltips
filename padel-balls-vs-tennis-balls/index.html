Padel balls vs tennis balls: what's the difference, and how long do they last?

Padel and tennis balls look almost identical. The differences are small on paper and obvious on court, especially when the ball comes off the back glass.

Quick answer: Padel balls are slightly smaller and have lower internal pressure than tennis balls, so they bounce lower and play slower. You can hit with tennis balls casually, but they fly off the glass too fast for proper padel.

The differences

Padel ballTennis ball
Diameter6.35 to 6.77 cmSlightly larger
Weight56.0 to 59.4 gSimilar
Internal pressureLowerHigher
BounceLower and slowerHigher and faster

Lower pressure is the big one. It lets the ball compress more against the racket, the turf and the glass, which keeps rallies under control in an enclosed court. Padel ball specifications are set by the International Padel Federation.

Can you use tennis balls for padel?

For a quick knock-about, yes. For a real game, no. A tennis ball bounces higher and comes off the back glass much faster, so balls you'd normally play off the wall end up out of reach. They also put more strain on your racket and arm.

How long do padel balls last?

Most padel balls are pressurised and start losing pressure the moment you open the tube. Casual players usually get a few sessions from a tube. Competitive players often open a new tube every match.

Replace your balls when:

  • they sound dull rather than crisp off the racket
  • they squeeze easily between your fingers
  • they bounce noticeably lower than a new ball
  • the felt has worn bald

How to make them last longer

  • Use a pressurised ball saver between sessions. It slows the decline but can't restore lost pressure.
  • Don't leave balls in a hot car.
  • Rotate tubes so you're not always playing with the oldest balls.

Frequently asked questions

Are padel balls smaller than tennis balls?

Slightly. Regulation padel balls measure 6.35 to 6.77 cm across, a touch smaller than tennis balls, and they have lower internal pressure.

Why do padel balls go flat so fast?

They're pressurised. Once the tube is opened, the gas inside slowly escapes through the rubber, and the bounce drops.
