Do you need eye protection for padel? What the research says

Eye doctors in several countries have noticed a rise in padel eye injuries. Here's what the research actually shows, and what it means for your game.

By Padel Tips editorial teamUpdated 31 October 20266 min read

On this page

  1. What the research found
  2. Why padel is risky for your eyes
  3. How serious are padel eye injuries?
  4. So should you wear eye protection?

Quick answer: Eye protection isn't compulsory, but it's a sensible choice. In a 2026 survey, about 1 in 10 padel players reported an eye injury, almost all caused by the ball, and the risk rose sharply for people who play several times a week.

What the research found

A 2026 study published in the journal Eye surveyed 583 padel players in Italy between April 2024 and March 2025.

10.3%reported at least one padel eye injury
92%of those injuries were caused by the ball
10%of injured players had eye surgery
25%of injured players reported a lasting effect on their quality of life

How often people played was the only factor that independently predicted injury. Players playing four to six matches a week were far more likely to report an injury than those playing less than once a week. Yet only about one in ten respondents strongly supported wearing goggles.

The study was a voluntary online survey, so it may over-represent players who've been injured. Even so, it lines up with earlier warnings. Ophthalmologists at the University of Gothenburg have described padel as a potential high-risk sport for eye injuries after eye clinics across Sweden saw a rise in cases.

Why padel is risky for your eyes

  • Close quarters. Four players share a 10 by 20 metre court. At the net you can be a few metres from a smash.
  • Rebounds. The ball comes back off the glass at angles that are hard to read.
  • The right-sized ball. A padel ball is small enough to fit into the eye socket and hit the eye directly.

How serious are padel eye injuries?

Most are mild, such as small bleeds inside the front of the eye that settle without surgery. Serious injuries do happen, including retinal tears and damage that permanently affects vision. That's why any eye injury with blurred vision, flashes, floaters or pain needs urgent attention.

So should you wear eye protection?

If you play often, play at the net a lot, or already have an eye condition, the case is strong. Protective eyewear is widely recognised as the most effective way to prevent sports eye injuries from balls. Modern sports glasses look far less like squash goggles than they used to.

Frequently asked questions

Is padel eye protection compulsory?

No. Neither the rules nor most clubs require it. It's a personal choice.

Who is most at risk of a padel eye injury?

In the 2026 study, people who played most often. Playing at the net, against hard hitters, also puts you closer to the ball.

What should I do if I'm hit in the eye?

Stop playing. Seek urgent help if you have blurred vision, flashes, floaters, a shadow in your vision, pain or blood in the eye. This is general information, not medical advice.
