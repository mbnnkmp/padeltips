Can you play padel in running shoes or tennis shoes?

For your first session, any clean trainers will get you on court. But if you're playing regularly, running shoes are the gear most likely to get you hurt.

Quick answer: You can wear running shoes for a first try, but they're a poor choice for regular padel. They lack sideways support and their soles slip on sandy turf. Clay-court tennis shoes are an acceptable stopgap. Padel shoes are best.

Why running shoes don't work for padel

Running shoes are designed for one direction: forwards. Padel is mostly sideways shuffles, sudden stops and quick changes of direction.

  • Soft, tall cushioning. Great for absorbing forward strides. On a lateral move it lets your foot roll over the edge of the sole, which is how ankles get sprained.
  • Little sideways support. Mesh uppers are light and breathable but don't hold your foot over the sole.
  • The wrong tread. Road-running soles slide on the sand spread over padel turf.
  • Fast wear. Dragging your toe on low shots wears through running-shoe mesh quickly.

What about tennis shoes?

Tennis shoes are a much better stopgap because they're built for lateral movement. Clay-court tennis shoes, with a full herringbone sole, grip sandy padel turf well. Hard-court tennis shoes have flatter treads and can feel slippery on a sandy court.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear trainers for padel?

For a first session, yes. For regular play, choose padel or clay-court tennis shoes, which have the lateral support and grip padel needs.

Are padel shoes the same as tennis shoes?

They're close. Padel shoes typically have soles tuned for sandy artificial turf and reinforced toes for low shots.

Do I need padel shoes as a beginner?

If you're playing more than occasionally, yes. Good shoes are one of the best ways to reduce the risk of ankle and knee injuries.
