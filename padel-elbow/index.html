Padel elbow: causes, prevention and the rackets that help

Padel elbow is padel's version of tennis elbow: pain on the outside of the elbow from overloaded forearm tendons. It's common, it's frustrating, and your racket plays a bigger part than you might think.

Quick answer: Padel elbow is usually caused by repetitive strain, made worse by a stiff or heavy racket, a tight grip and poor technique. Switching to a soft-core, lighter racket, relaxing your grip and building up playing time gradually all help. Persistent pain needs a physio or doctor.

What is padel elbow?

Padel elbow is lateral epicondylitis, the same condition as tennis elbow. The tendons that attach your forearm muscles to the bony bump on the outside of your elbow become irritated from repeated strain. It typically hurts when you grip, lift or twist, such as when you open a jar or pick up a kettle.

It's common. In one survey of recreational players in Italy, the elbow was the second most common injury site after the lower leg and Achilles tendon.

Why padel is hard on the elbow

  • No strings. A padel racket is solid, so more of the shock from each hit travels into your arm than with a strung tennis racquet.
  • Lots of volleys and blocks. Padel is played close to the net, with many short, sharp contacts.
  • Mishits. Off-centre hits send more vibration into the arm. Beginners mishit more often.
  • Gripping too tightly. A death grip keeps your forearm muscles tense through every shot.
  • Too much, too soon. Suddenly playing several times a week overloads tendons that haven't adapted.

Racket weight isn't a simple rule, though. A racket that's too light can encourage a harder, wristier swing. The Italian study above linked injury risk with players who didn't know their racket's weight or used rackets in the 356 to 375 g range, so the right racket is one that suits your strength and technique.

Padel rackets don't have strings, so the vibration dampeners tennis players use don't apply. Comfort comes from the core, the frame and the grip.

Other ways to prevent padel elbow

  • Relax your grip. Hold the racket firmly enough to control it, and no tighter.
  • Refresh your overgrip. A slippery grip makes you squeeze harder.
  • Build up gradually. Increase how often you play over several weeks.
  • Get a lesson. Late, wristy contact is a common cause. A coach can spot it in minutes.
  • Strengthen your forearm. Simple wrist and grip exercises help tendons cope with load.

When to get help. If elbow pain lasts more than a couple of weeks, wakes you at night, or makes everyday tasks hard, see a physiotherapist or GP. This article is general information, not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is padel elbow the same as tennis elbow?

Yes. Both are lateral epicondylitis, irritation of the tendons on the outside of the elbow. Padel players just call it padel elbow.

Can I keep playing padel with tennis elbow?

Many players can keep playing with lighter loads, a softer racket and an elbow strap, but pain that gets worse is a sign to stop and get it assessed.

Does an elbow strap help?

A counterforce strap worn just below the elbow can reduce load on the tendon for some players. It eases symptoms but doesn't fix the cause.
