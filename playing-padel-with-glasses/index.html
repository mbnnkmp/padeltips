How to play padel if you wear glasses

Plenty of padel players wear glasses. The question is how to see clearly without putting a breakable frame between your eye and a fast-moving ball.

Quick answer: Avoid playing in everyday glasses, which can break on impact. The safest options are contact lenses with protective goggles, goggles designed to fit over glasses, or prescription sports eyewear made to a racket-sport standard.

Why everyday glasses aren't safe

Normal glasses aren't made to take an impact. A ball can break the lens or frame, or push the frame into your face. That can turn a bruise into a cut or a more serious eye injury. Even polycarbonate everyday lenses usually sit in frames that aren't designed for sport.

Three safer options

1. Contact lenses plus protective eyewear

If you can wear contact lenses, this is the simplest route. Wear lenses and add any certified protective glasses, such as our recommended padel eyewear.

2. Goggles that fit over glasses

Some racket-sport goggles are designed to fit over your own glasses. They're affordable, but check the fit carefully. They need to sit securely without pressing your glasses into your face.

3. Prescription sports eyewear

Many sports goggles accept prescription lenses, either clipped in behind the main lens or as a direct prescription. An optician can fit these. It's the best long-term option if you play often and can't wear contacts.

What to check before you buy

  • Certification to ASTM F3164 or ASTM F803.
  • Polycarbonate lenses and a wrap-around frame.
  • An anti-fog coating or vents. Glasses under goggles fog easily indoors.
  • A strap or secure arms.

Frequently asked questions

Can you play padel with glasses on?

Many people do, but everyday glasses can break on impact. Protective goggles over your glasses, contacts with goggles, or prescription sports eyewear are safer.

Do padel goggles come in prescription?

Many racket-sport goggles take prescription lenses or an insert. An optician can fit them.
