Padel serve rules: how to serve legally in 2026

The padel serve is underarm and simple, but it's full of small rules that beginners break without realising. Here's how to serve legally, including the 2026 rule change.

Quick answer: Stand behind the service line, drop the ball so it bounces once, and hit it underarm at or below waist height diagonally into the opponent's box. Since January 2026, FIP rules require only one foot behind the line when you hit.

A legal serve, step by step

  1. Start on the right. Each game starts with a serve from the right side. Alternate sides after every point.
  2. Stand behind the service line on your side of the centre line.
  3. Drop the ball so it bounces once behind the service line. Don't throw it up.
  4. Hit at or below waist height, after the bounce.
  5. Aim diagonally into the opponent's service box.

The 2026 foot rule

Under the International Padel Federation rules in force from 1 January 2026, you only need one foot behind the service line when you hit the ball. You still can't touch the service line or step into the court before contact. Clubs and leagues may run their own rules, so check if you're not sure.

Two faults in a rowYou lose the point

Lets

A serve that clips the net and then lands in the correct box is a let. You replay that serve without penalty. A let on your second serve means you replay the second serve only.

Receiving the serve

The receiver must let the serve bounce before returning it. Volleying the serve loses the point. After the bounce, the receiver can also let the ball come off the glass before playing it.

Serving tips

  • Consistency beats power. An underarm serve won't win many points outright. Its job is to start the rally on your terms.
  • Serve and move forward. The serving pair wants to take the net.
  • Aim deep or at the body. A deep serve or one aimed at the receiver's hip is hard to attack.
  • Add slice later. Backspin keeps the ball low after it comes off the glass.

Frequently asked questions

Can you serve overarm in padel?

No. The ball must be hit at or below waist height after bouncing.

Does a padel serve have to bounce?

Yes. You drop the ball and let it bounce once behind the service line before hitting it.

Can the serve hit the glass?

Yes, after it bounces in the correct box. If it hits the metal fence after bouncing, it's a fault.

What changed in the 2026 padel serve rules?

The FIP now requires only one foot behind the service line at contact, rather than both.
