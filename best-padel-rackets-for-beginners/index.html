What a beginner racket needs

  • A round shape for the biggest, most central sweet spot. See racket shapes explained.
  • A soft core that helps the ball off the face on slower shots and absorbs vibration.
  • A low weight, so you can react quickly at the net and your arm doesn't tire.
  • A fibreglass face, which is more flexible and forgiving than carbon.

What to avoid

  • Pro rackets. They're stiff, head-heavy and built for perfect technique.
  • Very cheap supermarket rackets. Low-grade foam can go dead within a few months.
  • Buying on looks or player endorsements. Choose on shape, weight and feel.

When to upgrade

Most players know within a year. If you're comfortable at the net, your technique on overheads is consistent and you want more finish on your smash, it's time to look at a teardrop. Our main racket guide has intermediate and advanced picks.

Frequently asked questions

What weight padel racket should a beginner use?

Lighter is usually better. Most adult rackets weigh roughly 350 to 375 grams, and beginners are typically happiest towards the lighter end.

Should a beginner buy a carbon padel racket?

It isn't necessary. Fibreglass faces are more flexible and forgiving, which suits beginners. Carbon adds stiffness and precision that pays off later.

Can I just rent a racket?

Yes. Most clubs rent rackets, and it's a good way to try a few shapes before you buy.
