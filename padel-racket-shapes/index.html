Padel racket shapes explained: round vs teardrop vs diamond

A racket's shape is the single biggest clue to how it will play. It sets where the weight sits, where the sweet spot is and how much the racket forgives a mistimed shot.

Quick answer: Round rackets give the most control and forgiveness and suit beginners. Teardrop rackets balance control and power for improving all-rounders. Diamond rackets give the most power on smashes but have a small sweet spot, so they suit experienced attackers.

Round rackets: control first

A round racket keeps its weight low, close to your hand. The sweet spot sits in the middle of the face and is the largest of the three shapes, so off-centre hits still go roughly where you meant them to.

  • Strengths: control, forgiveness, quick hands at the net, easier on the arm.
  • Trade-off: less natural power on overheads.
  • Best for: beginners, defensive players and anyone with elbow or shoulder trouble.

Teardrop rackets: the all-rounder

A teardrop narrows towards the throat and has a medium balance. The sweet spot is slightly higher than on a round racket. You get a bit more power without giving up much control.

  • Strengths: versatility. It defends well and still finishes points.
  • Trade-off: it doesn't excel at either extreme.
  • Best for: improving players buying their second racket, and all-court players at any level.

Diamond rackets: power first

A diamond racket is widest high up the face and has a head-heavy balance. That extra mass at the top adds power to smashes and bandejas. The sweet spot is small and high, so mistimed shots are punished.

  • Strengths: power on overheads.
  • Trade-off: harder to control, slower at the net and harder on the elbow.
  • Best for: experienced attacking players with sound technique.

Which shape should you choose?

Be honest about where you win and lose points. If most of your errors are mishits from the back of the court, a round racket will win you more points than a diamond ever will. If you're consistently at the net and your smashes are being retrieved, a teardrop or diamond can add the finish you're missing.

Shape isn't the whole story, though. Core hardness, face material and weight also change the feel. A soft-core diamond can feel more forgiving than a stiff round racket. Our best padel rackets guide covers all four factors, with picks for each level.

Frequently asked questions

Which padel racket shape is best for beginners?

Round. It has the largest, most central sweet spot and the lowest balance, which makes it the most forgiving and the easiest to control.

Is a diamond racket better?

Only for the right player. Diamond rackets give the most power but the least control. Most club players hit more winners with a round or teardrop racket.

What is a hybrid padel racket?

Brands sometimes use "hybrid" for teardrop-style rackets that blend round and diamond characteristics. Check the balance point and sweet spot description rather than the label.
