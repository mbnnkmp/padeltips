The best padel rackets, and why

The best racket is not the one the pros use. It's the one that matches your level. Beginners want forgiveness and comfort. Advanced players want power and precision. Start with the shape.

Round = controlLow balance and the biggest sweet spot. Best for beginners and defensive players.
Teardrop = all-roundA mid balance that mixes control with some power. A good second racket.
Diamond = powerA head-heavy balance with a smaller sweet spot. Best for experienced attackers.
Mind your elbowSofter cores and fibreglass faces are kinder to your arm than stiff carbon.

The best padel shoes, and why

Running shoes are the most common beginner mistake. Padel is all sprints, stops and sideways moves on sandy artificial turf. You need a shoe made for that.

Herringbone or omni soleThis tread grips sand-filled turf without locking your foot in place, which is how ankles and knees get hurt.
Lateral supportA firm upper and heel stop your foot rolling on quick side-steps.
Reinforced toeLow volleys drag your toe across the turf. A reinforced toe stops it wearing through.
Replace them on timeRegular players wear through a pair in 6–12 months, and worn soles lose grip.

Padel and eye injuries: the risk nobody talks about

Padel puts four players in a small, enclosed court. The ball is fast, and it often rebounds off the glass at unexpected angles. At the net, you're only a few metres from a full-power smash. Eye doctors in several countries have noticed.

1 in 10players in a 2026 Italian survey reported at least one padel eye injury
92%of those injuries were caused by the ball
Up to 90%lower risk of eye injury when proper protective eyewear is worn correctly

Most padel eye injuries are minor, such as small bleeds inside the eye that heal without surgery. Serious cases do happen, though, including retinal tears, a dislocated lens or, rarely, permanent vision loss. The more often you play, the higher your risk. The same Italian survey found frequent players were far more likely to report an injury.

What to look for in padel eyewear. There is no padel-specific standard yet, so look for racket-sport eyewear certified to ASTM F3164 or the older ASTM F803. That's the standard used for squash. You also want polycarbonate lenses, a wrap-around frame and a secure strap or grip arms. Ordinary sunglasses and prescription glasses are not protective, and they can shatter on impact. If you wear glasses, look for goggles that fit over them or that accept prescription lenses.
