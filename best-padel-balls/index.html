What makes a good padel ball

  • Consistency. All three balls in a tube should bounce the same. Cheap balls often don't.
  • Speed. "Fast" balls suit attacking players and warm conditions. Standard balls are easier to control.
  • Durability. Better balls hold their pressure and felt for longer, which matters more than the price per tube.
  • FIP approval. If you play competitions, check the ball is on the International Padel Federation's approved list.

Those small differences matter. A livelier tennis ball flies off the back glass too fast for controlled rallies. Read more in padel balls vs tennis balls.

How to tell your balls are dead

Pressurised balls start losing pressure as soon as you open the tube. Replace them when they sound dull off the racket, squeeze softly, or bounce noticeably lower than a new ball. Cold weather makes every ball feel slower, so warm balls up before an outdoor winter game.

Frequently asked questions

Are padel balls the same as tennis balls?

No. Padel balls are slightly smaller and have lower internal pressure, so they bounce lower and travel slower. That's what makes long rallies off the glass possible.

Can you use tennis balls for padel?

You can for a casual knock-about, but they bounce too high and come off the glass too fast. Use proper padel balls for real games.

How long do padel balls last?

A tube of good balls usually lasts a few matches for casual players. Competitive players often open a new tube every match. Replace them when they lose their bounce.

What are the fastest padel balls?

Of our picks, the Head Padel Pro S is the fastest. Fast balls suit attacking players and are used in pro events.
