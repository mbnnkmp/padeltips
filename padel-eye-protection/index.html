Padel eye protection: the best goggles and glasses

Padel puts four players in a small, enclosed court with a fast ball that rebounds off the glass at unexpected angles. At the net, you're only a few metres from a full-power smash. Here's how to protect your eyes, and which eyewear to buy.

What to look for in padel eyewear

There's no padel-specific eyewear standard yet, so use the racket-sport standards instead.

  • Certification. ASTM F3164 is the current US standard for racket-sport eye protectors (racquetball, squash, tennis and pickleball). The older ASTM F803 is still common on squash goggles. Check the listing or packaging for the standard, not just the word "protective".
  • Polycarbonate lenses. Polycarbonate is highly impact-resistant and won't shatter like glass.
  • Wrap-around frame. Balls coming off the side glass don't always arrive head-on.
  • A secure fit. A strap or grippy arms stop them moving when you lunge.
  • Anti-fog vents or coating. Indoor courts get warm. Eyewear that fogs ends up in your bag.

Sunglasses and normal glasses are not eye protection. Their lenses and frames can break on impact and cause a worse injury. If you wear glasses, read how to play padel in glasses.

If you're hit in the eye

Stop playing. If you notice blurred or reduced vision, flashes, new floaters, a shadow across your vision, pain, or blood in the eye, see an eye doctor or go to A&E straight away. Some serious injuries, like retinal tears, don't hurt much at first. This guide is general information, not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need eye protection for padel?

It isn't compulsory, but it's sensible, especially if you play often or at the net. Most padel eye injuries are caused by the ball, and certified protective eyewear is the most effective way to prevent them.

Are squash goggles OK for padel?

Yes. Squash goggles certified to ASTM F3164 or ASTM F803 are designed for a fast, hard ball in an enclosed court, which makes them a good fit for padel.

Can I wear sunglasses to play padel outdoors?

Ordinary sunglasses aren't protective and can shatter on impact. If you want tinted lenses, choose certified sports eyewear with tinted polycarbonate lenses.

Can I play padel in my normal glasses?

It's better not to. Look for goggles that fit over glasses, or protective eyewear that takes prescription lenses. See playing padel with glasses.
